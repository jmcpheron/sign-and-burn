# 2026-10-10: scanning an approval's codes in the page

## The ask

Instead of one dense QR code that is a link, a payload in the codes, and a button on the paying page
that opens the camera and reads it. Two choices were put to Jason, and he chose: pin jsQR for the
reading, and split the approval into several smaller codes.

## Why it helps

- The paying device can open signandburn.app in its wallet app's own browser and scan there. A
  phone's own camera opens a link in Safari, where the wallet usually isn't.
- A version 37-38 code (165-169 modules) is hard to read. Five version 18 codes (89 modules) are easy.
- The payload can carry what a link didn't: Jason's screenshot showed an approval for a Safe{Wallet}
  transaction that also needs `0x1289…`'s off-chain signature. The link carries the approval alone,
  so a wallet that sent it would land the seat's vote and leave the transfer for "Run it". The codes
  carry the signature, and the one send runs it.

## What changed

- **`sign-and-burn/approval-qr/v2`** (`src/pay.mjs`): the compact approval's bytes (format 1's
  fields), then up to 10 owners' signatures (20 + 65 bytes each), in base 43, cut into about 5 parts:
  `SAB2/<part>/<parts>/<id>/<base 43>`. id is the first 8 hex digits of the payload's SHA-256: parts of
  another approval start over, and the whole is checked once put together.
- **Both pages' share boxes** show "Show QR codes": the parts cycling twice a second, with Pause and
  Next, and say they are for the site's scanner, not a phone's camera. They carry the Safe{Wallet}
  signatures kept with that transaction (`pay.keptSigs`).
- **"Scan a code"** under "Pay for a request" on the main page (`src/scan.mjs`): the camera, read by
  jsQR, about eight looks a second. Parts are put together in any order (`pay.partCollector`); a link's
  code is read as a pasted link; a wallet-page link goes to the wallet page. Nothing from the camera is
  kept or sent.
- **The paying page** checks each signature that came with an approval: it must recover to its owner,
  an owner of the Safe, for the hash its own console works out. It counts the votes the Safe holds on
  chain too, says how many that makes, and its one send carries them all.
- **jsQR 1.4.0** (`jsqr`, Apache-2.0, no dependencies), pinned exactly and by its hash in the
  lockfile, the one package added. It is bundled into app.js, about 340 KB more; its license goes in
  `docs/vendor/jsqr/`, and NOTICE names it. The page still loads nothing from anywhere else, and the
  CSP is unchanged.

The console and the contracts are unchanged.

## What was checked, and how

- `npm test`: the parts round-trip with a signature, in any order, twice over; each is a version 18
  code; text that isn't a part is ignored; another approval's part starts over; a changed part is
  refused.
- `npm run e2e`: all 153 checks pass. Chromium's fake camera plays a video of the page's own codes
  (YUV4MPEG2, written by the test), and a third browser's "Scan a code" reads them through jsQR into the
  approval. Then the case from the screenshot: a Safe{Wallet} transaction on a seat device with no
  wallet, its codes carrying the wallet's signature, scanned and sent from another browser, and the
  Safe runs it in that one transaction.

Not checked: a real phone's camera on a real screen.

## Found along the way

- The main page's amount, typed by the e2e, was lost once: a redraw landed between Playwright's select
  and its typing. The page redraws its screen on chain events, and replaces the form's inputs as it
  does; a person typing at that moment could lose a keystroke too. The e2e now checks each value it
  types into the main page's form, and types it again if it didn't take. Not fixed in the page: that
  wants a screen that doesn't rebuild a form being typed in.
