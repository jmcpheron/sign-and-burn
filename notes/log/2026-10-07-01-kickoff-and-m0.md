# 2026-10-07, evening: the kickoff, and M0

Times are US Pacific.

## The ask

The repository was PicoQuorum's: a hardware console that is one owner of a Safe. The ask was to turn
it into Sign and Burn, from a kickoff document written the same evening after Justin Drake's
"bunker mode" post: a Safe owner that signs with a one-time key and burns it, a passkey as the key, a
browser console, and a "shielded Safe". Keep the stack simple enough to run on a Pico or an ESP32, and
say so on the page. Show a history of what it did.

Three decisions, asked and answered first:

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| PicoQuorum's hardware, build log, app and site | **Delete them.** Git history keeps them |
| Foundry for the contracts (a new dependency) | **Install it, pinned** |
| The console | **Full size in the browser, on the same MicroPython firmware**, with a "could run on an ESP32" label and a history panel |

## M0: the one-time keys (about 23:20)

- `reference/python/sign_and_burn.py`: Winternitz, w = 16, 67 chains, SHA-256, a tweak per step.
  Standard library only.
- `reference/js/sign-and-burn.mjs`: the same, with node's own crypto.
- `reference/vectors/generate.py` wrote `v1.json`: 7 cases with every intermediate value, including
  the two extreme checksums (960 and 0) and the largest usable `n`.
- Both implementations agreed on all 7. Later the vectors were rewritten as `0x`-prefixed hex, so
  Foundry and viem read them as is; a script confirmed every value was unchanged.
