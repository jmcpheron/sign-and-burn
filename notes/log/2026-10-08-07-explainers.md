# 2026-10-08, evening: three explainers in "How it works"

## The ask

The page explains a lot in words. Draw more of it. Of nine mockups, three went in: one tap's two
jobs, one hash chain up close, and why a key signs only once.

## What changed

- **One tap, two jobs, step by step.** Plain HTML and CSS: the tap, what the passkey returns, what
  the console makes from it, and what checks it on chain. No script.
- **A one-time key, up close.** A real chain of a throwaway key, drawn by `src/main.mjs` with
  `src/wots.mjs`: secret below the revealed position, values anyone can compute above it. Below
  that, all 67 positions of a real signature; pick one to see its chain. The page checks the
  signature against the key's fingerprint and says so.
- **Why a key signs only once.** One, two or four signatures by that throwaway key, drawn with the
  danger case's canvas. It shows the lowest position each chain gave away, and the forger's odds per
  try from `wots.chance`, worked out for those signatures.

The throwaway key is made from a fixed label in the page, never from a passkey, and never goes on
chain. No new dependency, no network call. The console is unchanged, so its fingerprint is too.

## What was checked, and how

- `npm test`, `npm run build`, `node tools/fingerprint.mjs --check`.
- `npm run e2e` with Foundry 1.8.3 and Chromium: all pass, with two new checks. One picks chain 64
  and expects a checksum digit and a good signature. The other picks four signatures and expects
  odds.
- Screenshots at 1360 and 390 px wide, in light and dark. No CSP errors and no sideways scroll.

## Found along the way

- With these four example messages, two signatures give about 1 in 1.1 × 10^11 per try and four give
  about 1 in 190,000. That's close to the figures in
  [one-time-keys.md](../concepts/one-time-keys.md) (about 1 in 10^10, and 1 in 150,000). They depend
  on the messages.
- At phone width, labels like "pos 10" didn't fit in a chain's cells. The cells now show only the
  number.
